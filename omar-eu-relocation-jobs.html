Visa routes — the numbers that matter

🇳🇱 Netherlands — Highly Skilled Migrant (kennismigrant). Threshold for under 30: €4,357/month gross (excl. 8% holiday allowance) ≈ €52.3k/yr. Age 30+: €5,942/month. Comfortably below Amsterdam market rate for your level, so the salary bar is not your obstacle — the obstacle is that only an IND recognised sponsor may legally file for you. Check every NL employer against the register before applying. The 30% ruling holds at 30% through 2026 and drops to 27% for new rulings from 1 Jan 2027 — landing a ruling this year is worth real money over five years. The government also announced a tightening of the whole scheme in July 2025 (not yet in force). Direction of travel is against you: move now.
🇩🇪 Germany — EU Blue Card. €50,700/yr standard, but software developers (ISCO 2512) are a shortage occupation → €45,934.20/yr applies to you. Every Berlin/Munich role on this list should clear that comfortably. Two important details: (1) if you hold a degree, verify it in anabin before applying — an unrecognised degree is a common silent rejection; (2) there is a no-degree IT route — €45,934.20 + 3 years relevant IT experience in the last 7 years — so you qualify on the experience track regardless. Processing is 8–12 weeks normally, but the Accelerated Skilled Worker Procedure runs 4–6 weeks and is employer-initiated. Ask any German employer to use it. Fallback if no offer: the Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte) gives you 12 months in-country to job-hunt; with a recognised BSc you qualify without the points test, need ~€13,092 in proof of funds, and may work 20 hrs/week.
🇩🇰 Denmark — use the Positive List, not the Pay Limit Scheme. The Pay Limit bar is DKK 552,000/yr (~€73,800) — the highest of the four countries, and probably out of reach at your level. But "Software Backend Developer" and "Programmer and System Developer" are both on the Positive List for People with a Higher Education, which has no fixed salary floor — only "customary Danish standards". Your BSc qualifies you. Most Danish hiring managers don't realise their own posting maps onto this route, so name it explicitly in your first email. Separately, ask whether the employer is SIRI Fast-track certified (1-month processing) — it's a great early screening question and the certified list is public. Fee DKK 6,810. Lists refresh 1 Jan / 1 Jul; the higher-education list shrank in July 2026, so re-check before each wave.
🇪🇸 Spain — easiest legally, hardest to discover. The Highly Qualified Professional route (Ley de Startups) is processed by UGE-CE in ~20 business days with no labour market test — the employer never has to prove no Spaniard could do the job. That makes it the cheapest and fastest of the four. But the salary floor is €41,356.36/yr and it is the binding constraint: mid-level Java salaries in Málaga or Valencia often sit below it. Barcelona and Madrid clear it more reliably. The under-30 reduction coefficient was abolished. Also: most Spanish employers don't know this route is cheap and fast — it is worth telling them in your cover letter. Trade-off: Spanish salaries run 35–45% below Northern European levels.

Strategy — what I'd actually do

1. Apply to Talan (Spain) and N26 Customer Identity (Berlin) today.
Talan is the only posting found anywhere that combines an explicit work-permit offer, an English-only language bar, and Camunda 8 — your rarest skill, straight from Flightright. N26's Customer Identity team is Keycloak/OAuth2 work with "relocation package with visa support" written into the posting. These two are the highest-probability applications on the entire list.
2. Lead every German application with your two Berlin employers.
Flightright and XSonardive already paid you. German hiring managers systematically discount remote-from-Cairo candidates — naming two Berlin companies in your first line neutralises that objection before it forms. This is your single biggest structural advantage and most candidates in your position don't have it.
3. Say the visa route out loud, by name.
"I qualify for the EU Blue Card under the shortage-occupation threshold of €45,934" reads as competent. "I would need visa sponsorship" reads as a problem. Same fact, opposite effect. Use the Blue Card for Germany, kennismigrant for NL, Positive List ("Software Backend Developer") for Denmark, HQP/UGE-CE for Spain.
4. Close the cloud-native gap.
Kubernetes and Terraform are described as table stakes for 2026 backend roles, not bonuses. Your CV has Docker, Jenkins and AWS but no K8s or IaC. A pure Spring Boot + React profile without them reads as slightly dated to European reviewers. Worth a CKAD or even a weekend project you can point to — it's the cheapest single upgrade to your hit rate.
5. Add a Kotlin line.
Roughly half the German and Danish JDs say "Java/Kotlin" or "JVM language". Your Java transfers directly, but reviewers pattern-match on the literal word. One honest line — "comfortable picking up Kotlin; the JVM ecosystem is the same" — costs nothing and unlocks a chunk of the list.
6. Sequence: Germany → Netherlands → Denmark → Spain.
Germany has the most volume and the friendliest shortage-occupation threshold. NL has the hardest legal gate but the highest salaries and a closing 30%-ruling window. Denmark's Positive List is a genuinely cheap door that few people use. Spain is easiest to get approved but hardest to find roles in, and pays least. Note the market shape: 2026 EU tech hiring is a barbell — juniors and seniors are moving, mid-level (3–5 yrs) is the slowest segment. At 6 years you're just above the slow zone; lean on the differentiators.
