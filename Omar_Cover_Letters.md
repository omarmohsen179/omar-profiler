## The one thing that makes these work

Every letter below opens on **Flightright and XSonardive**. That is deliberate. The unspoken objection to a Cairo-based applicant is *"can this person actually work in a European team, on European hours, to European standards?"* — and two Berlin companies who already paid you for eighteen months of production work answers it before it gets asked. Most candidates in your position cannot make that claim. Lead with it every single time.

The second deliberate choice: **name the visa route, don't ask for help with it.** "I qualify for the EU Blue Card under the shortage-occupation threshold" reads as a competent person who has done their homework. "I would need visa sponsorship" reads as a problem to be solved. Same fact, opposite effect on the reader.

## The screening question to ask first

When a recruiter replies, ask this before investing in interview rounds:

> *"Before we go further — is this role open to a candidate relocating from outside the EU, and has {COMPANY} sponsored a work permit before? I qualify for {ROUTE} and I'm happy to talk through how it works, but I'd rather know early if it's a blocker."*

It costs you nothing, and it saves you the C Teleport situation — three interview rounds ending in *"we don't relocate candidates currently based outside the country."*

## Two honest gaps, and what to do about them

**Kubernetes and Terraform.** European backend postings in 2026 treat these as table stakes rather than bonuses. You have Docker, Jenkins and AWS, which is most of the way there — but "Spring Boot + React, no K8s" reads as slightly dated to a European reviewer scanning quickly. This is the cheapest single upgrade to your hit rate: a CKAD, or even a deployed side project you can point to. Don't claim it until it's true.

**Kotlin.** Roughly half the German and Danish JDs say "Java/Kotlin" or "any JVM language". Your Java transfers directly and every one of those postings knows it — but reviewers pattern-match on the literal word. The line already in your CV ("happy to pick up Kotlin, which shares the JVM ecosystem I already work in daily") is honest and does the job. Say it out loud in interviews too.
